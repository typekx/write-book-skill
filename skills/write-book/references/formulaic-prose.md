# Editing formulaic prose

The author may call these problems "AI mannerisms". Treat this as editing observable features. They also occur in texts by people; they do not identify authorship or promise any detector result. The goal is better prose and a preserved voice. The examples are in English; the same patterns exist in other languages.

## Catalogue of candidates

| Pattern | Symptom and effect | Possible correction |
|---|---|---|
| Argumentative enumeration | "Firstly, secondly", "another aspect is" turn a scene into a lecture. | Keep the line of decision, remove needless labels. In a document or an analyst's dialogue an enumeration may be natural. |
| Explaining a thought | Thought, gesture, narrator's comment and punchline explain the same fear. | Keep the carrier that adds most; keep the thought if it changes the interpretation or the decision. |
| Paraphrase without increment | The next paragraph repeats the previous one without changing the stakes. | Merge or cut; the next step of reasoning needs a new premise or choice. |
| Serial antithesis | "It wasn't X. It was Y.", "not because…, but because…" replace the development of a thought. | Say the essential thing directly or show the difference; keep a deliberate contrast. |
| Automatic triads | Every description has three nouns, gestures or short sentences. | Choose the number of elements by meaning, not rhetorical ritual. |
| Abstract punchline | Scene after scene ends with a thesis about truth, price, silence or limits. | End on the consequence; let it resonate without explanation. |
| Paragraph punchline | A short "And that was enough." or "Nothing more." closes a paragraph mid-chapter. | End the paragraph with information; leave resonance for the end of the chapter. |
| Colon reveal | "He knew one thing: …", "There was only one thing: …". | A sentence without the announcement. |
| Anaphoric enumeration | "no…, no…, no…", "without…, without…, without…", "he didn't ask, didn't call, didn't wait". | Two members or a different syntax. |
| Artificial suspense | "He didn't know yet", "nothing would ever be the same" without support in the scene. | Base expectation on the present conflict; respect the narrator's convention. |
| Repeated gesture | Jaw, fists, breath and trembling hands serve every emotion. | Fit the expression to the character; do not swap one gesture for a random substitute. |
| Forced sensory detail | Every opening has coffee, rain or light without meaning. | Keep detail that comes from attention and action; drop decorative filler. |
| Uniform dialogue | Everyone is eloquent, therapeutic and names themselves accurately. | Restore differences of goals, register, knowledge, evasions and self-awareness. |
| Instructional dialogue | Speakers explain known facts to each other for the reader's convenience. | Change the information situation or limit it to the real purpose of the conversation. |
| Smooth reconciliation | One conversation removes a long concealment or loss of trust. | Keep the consequences and partial agreement, without artificially adding quarrels. |
| Synonym rotation | A fixed object receives ever different, unnatural labels. | Restore clarity; names and evidential terms may need repeating. |
| Stacked metaphors | Mixed images or associations outside the character's experience. | Choose an image consistent with the voice; consider a plain sentence. |
| Empty qualifier | "In a way", "somehow", "as if", "something in him" blur most observations. | Distinguish a character's real uncertainty from imprecise writing. |
| Excessive symmetry | Constant lengths and the order gesture–thought–punchline in every scene. | Vary by meaning and tension, without randomly breaking the rhythm. |
| Too many reminders | Every chapter summarises the past and explains the motivation again. | Keep the reminder the current decision needs. |
| Fake concreteness | Times, numbers and expertise without source or function. | Check origin and usefulness; do not add precision for the sound of it. |

## Disguises, replacements and project limits

**Disguises.** A removed construction returns with different syntax. "It wasn't X. It was Y." turns into "Not X. Y.", "This was no accident. It was a choice.", "It had never been about the money. It had been about…". Antithesis hides in "not because…, but because…" and in sentences with "only". Look for the pattern, not the word.

**Replacement tic.** Replacing a banned phrase with one synonym creates a new tic. Removing one verb can make its replacement appear in every other chapter. Strings of dialogue tags such as "without pausing", "without anger", "she said calmly" arise in the same way. After editing, count the replacements as you counted the original, and vary the solutions: different syntax, a concrete verb, removing a needless comment.

**Project limits.** If the author set numbers per chapter (e.g. zero "It wasn't X. It was Y." pairs, at most one antithesis per chapter), enforce them with a counter ([technical check](technical-check.md)) and by reading. A limit does not exempt you from judging function: a hit within the limit may still be unnecessary, and a false alarm is described in the report instead of being rewritten. Record deliberate exceptions (motifs, quoted documents, characters' voices in dialogue) in the style guide so that editing does not remove them.

**The cost of late editing.** Reworking a finished volume for mannerisms involves hundreds of paragraphs. It is cheaper to put the limits into the instructions before writing the next volume and to check every unit.

## Repeated-thought test

Trace: stimulus → thought → action → narrator's comment → punchline. What does each link add? Repetition can show obsession, denial, memory or a change of understanding. Without such a function it is a candidate for editing.

Working example:

> He put the phone down. He didn't want to call back because he was afraid of the answer. The fear made him hesitate. He knew this conversation could change everything.

Possible cut:

> He put the phone face down on the counter. He was afraid of hearing her answer.

The cut works if the expected answer is clear from context and the removed sentences hold no needed premise. Another style may need the extended monologue kept and only the paraphrase removed.

## Procedure

1. Establish the style model and the kind of passage: narration, dialogue, thought, document or report. Do not judge them by one measure.
2. Mark the occurrences and their accumulation in the unit and neighbouring chapters. Give the fragment and location.
3. Decide: deliberate device, excess or uncertain case. A list of hits is not a list of errors.
4. Within the edit, apply the smallest effective change. Adding a motivation or a fact is a plot change and needs the corresponding scope.
5. Check style, immersion and canon after the change. Removing a phrase is not enough if the pattern remains.

Do not introduce a global ban on enumerations, reflection, adverbs and metaphors. Do not add errors for apparent naturalness. Motifs and deliberate refrains may recur more often than accidental phrases of the narrator.

## Thinning tics in a volume and a series

The tics of a finished volume (gestures, adverbs, narrative connectives) are counted in hundreds. Work like this:

1. **Measure density per 10,000 words** in each volume of the series (`wb density`). If the author wants an even style, the target is the leanest volume, not zero. Example from practice: "Then" at the start of a sentence ran at 7–8 per 10,000 words in two volumes and 19–29 in three others; after levelling all were within 7–10.
2. **List the hits with context** (`wb ticks`). For each one decide:
   - it stays, because it carries meaning ("She did not nod.", a one-word "Once.", a signal gesture in an interrogation);
   - it is replaced (a concrete verb, different syntax);
   - it is deleted.

   In practice, cutting "nodded" from 58 to 19 kept every gesture that had a function.
3. **Mechanical rules only with exceptions,** when the scale forces automation (e.g. hundreds of "Then"):
   - never inside dialogue: dialogue is the character's voice;
   - do not delete a word if the sentence would then start with a pronoun or particle that cannot stand first in that language;
   - leave short sentences in which the word is the content ("Then nothing.");
   - keep sequential pairs ("First… Then…");
   - keep at most one per paragraph, and remove every second of the rest until the density nears the target.

   Save a backup before the pass, then review a sample of changes in context and compare with the backup to make sure the rule did not reach into dialogue.
4. **Check the speaker after cuts.** Removing "X nodded." between lines can hand the next sentence to another person, especially when two people of the same gender are in the scene. After each deletion read the next sentence; if the subject became unclear, add the name or keep the gesture.
5. **A motif is not a tic.** A word that is the theme of the volume (e.g. "scope" in a volume about selecting documents, "she wrote it down" for a heroine who notes everything) is thinned only where it is narrator's jargon, not where it carries the motif.
