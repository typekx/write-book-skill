# Validating units and series

A check reveals contradictions and dependencies. Its scope, the sources read and the steps not taken are part of the result; an automatic tool does not confer literary quality.

## Identifiers and statuses

Link volume, unit, scene, fact and seed with stable identifiers, e.g. `V02-C07-S03`, `F-012`, `S-008`. These are examples, not a requirement to change existing numbering. A short quotation and the title help find the place after line numbers change.

Distinguish: proposal, accepted plan, written, checked within a stated scope, changed and abandoned. An accepted plan is not a written fact. An empty field may mean no check was done, not that there is no problem.

## Five levels

| Moment | Scope |
|---|---|
| Before the first volume | Series architecture, conditions of the ending, chronology, arcs and reveal dependencies. |
| Before a unit | Entry state, knowledge, motivation, props, style variant and conditions of future payoffs. |
| After a unit | Facts, consequences, style, immersion, mannerisms, length and the state record. |
| After a module | Transitions, flow of information, pace, repeated situations and thoughts, relationships and reports. |
| After a volume | Local promise, debts to the reader, seeds for the future, the state for the next volume and backward consistency. |

## Classes of continuity errors

These categories usually produce most corrections after a volume is written. Check them deliberately, by searching the whole range, not only by reading:

| Class | How to search | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Relative time references | list "yesterday", "a week ago", "since Saturday", "for a month", "the first in two weeks" and recompute from the story calendar | "a week ago" although twelve days have passed |
| Recurring facts | search every repeated detail (frequency, address, floor, injury and which side, where an object is, who knows a password) | rent paid "monthly" in one chapter and "weekly" in another |
| Knowledge leak | scenes in which a character recalls a detail known only from a prologue, an interlude or another point of view | a character quotes a letter the reader saw only in the prologue |
| Unintended echo | the same description attached to two characters may suggest they are one person | the same distinctive smell on a neighbour and on an unknown culprit |
| Double presence | the same character in two places at the same time | a character at the station and at the office at the same hour |
| Character memory | whether a character remembers the same thing in different chapters | first cannot recall a conversation, later quotes it word for word |
| Who brought, who found | authorship of actions involving evidence and documents | one character found a document; later the credit goes to another |
| Forms of address | first names, titles, formal or informal address, language of the conversation | switching to first names earlier than the scene that establishes it |
| Weekday and age | calendar and timeline | a weekday that does not match the date |

## Independent review

Whoever wrote the text tends to read what they meant to write. After a module or volume, if possible, ask a separate agent or session that did not see the writing to review it, giving it the canon, the text and the list of error classes. Fresh eyes usually find small contradictions that self-checking missed. If that is not possible, run the scripts and read the riskiest places (the part written earliest, scenes with many facts from earlier volumes) and record this limitation in the report.

After the ending, check the resolutions backwards: were the premises prepared, has the meaning of a fact been changed without grounds, was a thread dropped by accident? Deliberate openness is acceptable when it matches the promise of the book.

## Impact of a change

1. State the old and new version and the author's decision or the basis for the correction.
2. Find the source of the fact, direct uses and indirect dependencies: knowledge, motivation, alibis, chronology and resolutions.
3. Mark the status of each dependency. Word searches do not find every dependency of meaning.
4. Correct texts and registers within the authorised scope. State the remaining effects explicitly; do not mark them as done.
5. Read the changed scenes with their entry and consequence; update the state, the future plan and the exports.

Use the [impact register](../assets/project-template/06_Control/VALIDATION_AND_CHANGE_IMPACT.md). Name the main source; do not keep competing copies of the canon.

## Weight of findings

- **Blocks the dependent scene:** contradictory identity, impossible chronology, missing source of knowledge or object.
- **Editing:** repeated motivation, artificial dialogue, loss of voice, weak transition.
- **Author's decision:** variant of the ending, intended openness, change of convention.
- **Technical:** typography, heading, duplicate file, inconsistent list.

Record a problem as: location → observation → source of comparison → effect → correction → result of the recheck. Writing an error into a report does not resolve it. A cosmetic flaw does not block independent work.
