# Analysing and preserving the author's style

Goal: recognise the author's working choices and keep them while writing and editing. Do not confuse style with a list of banned words or with an identical rhythm in every paragraph.

## Sample and confidence

Passages the author names as a model take precedence. Otherwise choose available scenes representing action, conversation, reflection and transition, covering different points of view. Separate the author's text from other people's examples and from earlier model proposals. The latest generated version is not automatically the model of the voice.

Record the sources, units and the scope of what was actually read. One passage characterises that passage, not the whole book. Without samples, propose a profile and possibly a sample within the ordered scope; mark the profile as designed.

## Profile dimensions

| Dimension | What to recognise | How to preserve |
|---|---|---|
| Narrator | Person, tense, distance, irony, commentary and range of knowledge | Keep the rule; do not copy a single sentence pattern. |
| Rhythm | Distribution of sentence and paragraph lengths, breath, pauses | Compare scenes of a similar type; a chase and mourning do not need the same average. |
| Syntax | Complexity, ellipsis, order of information, repetition | Keep the tension between sentences and deliberate unevenness. |
| Vocabulary | Register, concreteness, expertise, regionalisms | Do not raise the register or replace plainness with ornate synonyms. |
| Imagery | Density of metaphor, fields of association, choice of senses | An image must fit the voice and the character's experience. |
| Thought and emotion | Monologue, free indirect speech, silences | Distinguish significant reflection from re-explaining readable behaviour. |
| Dialogue | Length of lines, subtext, evasions, register of characters | Do not give everyone the same eloquence and punchlines. |
| Exposition | How facts and the past are delivered | Information should arise from the scene's need or the narrative convention. |
| Openings and endings | Types of entry, punchlines, open endings | Control seriality without a mandatory surprise in every scene. |
| Typography | Dialogue, documents, thoughts, dates | Keep the convention and distinguish documents from narration. |

In the [style profile](../assets/project-template/01_Project_Rules/STYLE_PROFILE.md) record the observation, location, a short example and confidence. Divide findings into: trait to keep, scene-dependent variant, mannerism to reduce, open question.

## From profile to text

Before a scene, choose the most important traits for that point of view and tension. Do not reproduce characteristic sentences as templates to be filled again and again. A character's voice may differ from the narrator's and evolve if the change follows from the text.

After writing, compare the passage with the model. Look for drift: longer exposition, uniform dialogue, more aphorisms, explained emotions or decorative metaphors. Point to the places and repair their function. The average sentence length alone is not enough.

After a correction, read the paragraph together with its neighbours. A cut can remove an important hesitation, a mistaken conclusion or the cause of a decision. Checking that the voice is preserved also covers what has disappeared.

## Style memory across volumes

Keep a version of the profile and pointers to short model scenes, not just labels like "dark, cinematic". At the start of a volume, define the constant traits and justified changes linked to the narrator or a character's development. An authorised evolution of style is not an error; record its scope and the point of transition.

Do not create a new norm from one device in a single scene. Do not average the whole series to the parameters of one volume.

A style guide agreed while editing a volume prevails over the plan of the next. Record in it the series' signature traits, constructions to avoid with limits, deliberate exceptions (motifs, refrains, characters' voices) and a short description of each important character's voice: register, sentence length, what they avoid, how they address others. When a character's voice is in doubt, their best scene in the canon prevails; cite it with the chapter number.

When several models work on one series, compare new units with the canon's model scenes, not with the previous version generated by another model; drift accumulates with every handover.
