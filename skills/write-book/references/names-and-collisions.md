# Proper names and collisions

Read when introducing a new name of a company, institution, place or character, when renaming in a finished text and before sending a volume to typesetting.

## Name catalogue

Keep a [name catalogue](../assets/project-template/03_World_and_Notes/name_catalogue.md): base form, the inflected or possessive forms used in the text, kind (person, company, place, object), first appearance, collision-test status and retired names. Decide foreign names once: inflected or not, italic or not, whether a translation appears in the text.

Check collisions within the cast: two characters with the same name (including minor ones from an earlier volume), names that are equivalents in another language (e.g. John and Johan), surnames that sound alike in the same scenes. Check a new character against the catalogue before naming them.

## Real entities

Do not give real names to churches, hospitals, companies or offices where something bad or fictional happens. Describe them without a name or use a fictional name after testing it. Real cities, streets and public institutions can appear as background, in line with the author's decisions and the legal notice.

## Collision test

No hits in one search engine does not prove there is no collision: a name may be a registered trademark in another country, and a surname may belong to well-known people in the city of the story. For every new name of a company, institution or place:

1. the identical name in a search engine, also in quotation marks and in transliteration;
2. the company register of the country of the story and of the country the name comes from (e.g. Companies House in the UK);
3. industry and city: a name in the same industry as the fictional entity is a high risk;
4. trademarks (e.g. EUIPO, USPTO, national patent offices);
5. for a place: whether it is a real place name.

Record the result with the date, sources and a risk rating (low, moderate, high). Repeat the test just before the volume goes to print. A search engine does not replace legal registers; state in the report what was not checked and leave final confirmation in the register to the author if you have no access. Without network access, mark the test as not done.

## Renaming across a series

1. Record the author's decision and a change map: old name (retired from now on) → new name, all forms, scope.
2. Find all occurrences in all volumes, plans, registers, cards, legal notices, cover texts and exports. Count hits per file.
3. Replace from the rarest names to the most frequent, checking context: a quotation in historical documentation may stay, prose and registers may not. Check grammatical forms and agreement around the replacement.
4. After replacing, search for the old names again (including stems without endings), also in copies of files outside the main project folder: loose copies of the bible from before the change keep the old names and easily end up in the next handoff package. Add them to the banned names in the project check rules so that forms returning from plans or other models are caught automatically.
5. Update the volume version, the version history and the legal notice. If the volume is published, the change needs an erratum or a new edition; agree this with the author.
6. Rebuild the exports and check whether the page count has changed; if so, recalculate the cover.

A common trap: one file holds two layers, old prose and new decisions quoting examples with old names. Separate the prose from the conversation log first, then replace.
