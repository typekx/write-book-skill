# Designing a series in advance

When a series is ordered, design its architecture before the main writing of the first volume. All volumes get a general plan; increase scene detail for the nearest one. An ordered sample does not require planning the whole series first.

## Three levels of decisions

- **Invariants:** truths of the world, the significant past, limits of the mechanism, conditions of the ending and promises that must not be cancelled by accident.
- **Working plan:** the road to those points; scenes and decisions may change after checking dependencies.
- **Open variants:** decisions not yet taken. Say when a choice becomes necessary. Do not write a seed as certain for several contradictory solutions at once.

In an existing series the written prose is the foundation. Reconstructing its dependencies does not prove that later ideas were planned from the start. Compare a continuation plan written from conversation memory with the prose and keep a register of plan–canon conflicts: what the plan said, what the text says, how it was resolved, status (resolved / question for the author).

## Architecture of the whole

Establish the genre and emotional promise, the question of the series, the protagonist's intended change and the kind of ending. Set the number of volumes or a range; with an open range, separate the fixed core from optional episodes.

Write out the real history of events before dividing it into reveals. In crime fiction distinguish what happened, what the documents say, what the characters believe and what the reader knows. A false lead needs a cause and the possibility of fair reinterpretation.

Each volume gets a [card](../assets/project-template/07_Series_Architecture/VOLUME_CARD.md):

- its own conflict, stakes, goal and question;
- an entry state following from the previous volume;
- key choices and irreversible effects;
- arcs of characters, relationships and the antagonist;
- local resolution and the debt left to the reader;
- seeds for the future and limits of reveals;
- the exit state and the reason the next volume exists;
- a variant of tone and rhythm compared with the other volumes.

A volume must not be only a bridge to a more attractive part. Escalation can concern responsibility, the price of decisions, trust and the extent of knowledge; it does not need a bigger catastrophe each time. Do not reset relationships, health and skills to repeat an old conflict.

## Arcs and dependencies

Break the series arcs into stages across volumes. A change has a trigger, an action, a cost and a lasting effect. A secondary character should not exist only to deliver information to the protagonist.

Keep a [matrix of seeds and reveals](../assets/project-template/07_Series_Architecture/SEEDS_AND_REVEALS.md). For each resolution, list the facts, objects, skills, relationships and events required earlier. A source of knowledge that does not exist is a missing dependency, not a ready solution.

Plan flashbacks and changes of point of view: when the reader has an advantage over a character and what may be suggested without revealing the answer too early. What the authors know is not automatically what the narrator of a scene knows.

## Series identifiers and registers

In a long series, named identifiers save mistakes in conversations, cards and reports. Example families (choose what fits the project):

| Family | Example | Contents |
|---|---|---|
| Author's decisions | DEC-07 | question, options, ruling, date, consequences |
| Plot twists | TW-04 | what is reversed, in which volume and unit, setup |
| Volume threads | TH-2-03 | local thread, opening and closing |
| Evidence and documents | EV-015 | origin, original and copies, holder, reliability class |
| Violence | VIO-06 | scene, victim, physical, psychological and procedural consequences |
| Moral decisions | MOR-05 | choice, cost, rejected alternative |
| Invariants | INV-03 | a truth of the series that must not be broken (e.g. "the protagonist never returns to the family home") |
| Reveal gates | RG-07 | what must happen before the reader or a character may learn something |

The author's decisions have one register with a status: open, recommendation, decided, implemented in the text. Two versions of rulings on the same questions are an organisational error: the author names the valid one and the other goes to the archive.

In crime fiction or thrillers keep reliability classes for evidence (from rumour, through an account and a copy, to a document secured in proceedings). Characters treat evidence according to its class: nobody quotes a rumour as fact, and a copy does not replace the original. A character running an investigation may have a fixed set of questions they ask of every document.

## State after a volume

After finishing a volume, prepare a short "state after the volume" document for whoever writes the next: what was written and how many words, a calendar, a table of characters' and the reader's knowledge, props, forms of address, important new facts, threads and twists carried out and postponed, series counters, open decisions with recommendations, lessons from writing. It does not replace the canon; in doubt, the prose prevails.

## Detailed plan and control

Divide the nearest volume into modules, units and scenes. Describe the later volumes concretely enough to check dependencies. Check the ending backwards: every essential answer has a setup. Then go forwards: every seed has meaning, a payoff or a deliberately left openness.

Before writing a dependent scene, settle the decisions that affect its facts. Do not hold up independent scenes because of a distant detail. After the volume, compare plan and execution and update the state and the requirements for the next volume.

Changing the ending or the culprit's identity requires checking the earlier evidence, motivation, narration and promises to the reader — not just swapping a name at the end.
