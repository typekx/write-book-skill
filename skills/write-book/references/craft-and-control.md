# Prose craft and quality control

Read before writing, editing or auditing. The parameters of the author's profile take precedence over the default quality criteria below.

When building or comparing a voice use [style analysis](style-analysis.md); for problems of reception, [immersion](immersion.md); for serial mannerisms, [editing formulaic prose](formulaic-prose.md). Check across volumes according to [series validation](series-validation.md).

## Scene and voice

Establish what the character wants in the scene, what stands in the way and what changes through their decision. A scene may rest on action, conversation, observation or reflection; do not force the same turning-point pattern on every one.

Lead the reader through concrete detail available to the chosen narrator. Keep the established tense, distance and range of point of view. An omniscient narrator and POV changes are fine if they belong to the project's convention; do not impose close third person on every book.

Distinguish characters' voices by what they avoid, what they notice, what they leave unsaid and how they build an utterance. Dialogue should not merely summarise information both speakers already know. Dose exposition according to the needs of the scene and the promise of the genre.

Emotion can come from decisions, gestures, thoughts and the reactions of others. Do not replace every feeling with a bodily reaction or add a sensory flourish to every paragraph. When editing, look for repetitive sentence rhythm, images, punchlines and constructions, but correct their effect in context, not their frequency alone.

Do not lengthen a chapter with paraphrase. When the ordered length is missing, develop a significant action, choice, relationship or consequence. If this requires a change of plan, record it within the author's latitude. First drafts usually come out shorter than the target; plan scenes so that there is margin.

## Point of view, knowledge and endings

- One point of view per scene, if so agreed. Within one character's point of view, show another character's thoughts from outside: a gesture, a word, behaviour. If a card asks you to show that another character is "counting in their head" and the scene is not theirs, show what can be seen.
- A project may keep a list of characters who are never a point of view (usually antagonists and characters whose secret is at stake). Do not describe their thoughts or feelings from inside.
- The narrator does not foreshadow ("he did not yet know that…") unless that is the book's convention.
- Names and information reserved for later volumes do not appear early, not even as an ordinary word that sounds the same (e.g. a common noun that matches a name reserved for later).
- Hook: the last sentence or sentences of the unit carry the meaning of the hook from the card. When the author phrased the hook verbatim, keep it almost unchanged.

## Violence, sex and content boundaries

Follow the boundaries agreed with the author and the register of violent scenes, if the project keeps one ([content boundaries template](../assets/project-template/01_Project_Rules/content_boundaries.md)). Violence has physical, psychological and procedural consequences in this or later scenes; check health after earlier violence at the start of each unit. Outside planned scenes, do not add violence for intensity.

The author may forbid placing two threads side by side, even through an image or a character's association (e.g. two tragedies the author does not want linked). Such a ban applies to every paragraph; check it in the final reading, because it arises unnoticed in a character's reflections. Show threads about harm, as agreed, through witnesses' words and documents, without details the author did not order.

A scene of moral decision shows a real, costly alternative; the reader sees what the protagonist gives up. If the series has a moral invariant for the protagonist (e.g. "will never lie to a friend"), crossing it is a turning point to be recorded in the state and acknowledged in the next volume.

## Project style

From the profile and representative scenes, read the genre, audience, language, narration, pace, tone, lengths and typography. Specific word bans, colon limits, minimum word counts or non-breaking spaces apply only where the author actually set them. Do not carry them over automatically from an earlier book.

Keep the dialogue and quotation conventions used in the manuscript consistently, according to the language of the book (e.g. curly quotes in English, „…” and dashes for dialogue in Polish). Normalise typography in a working copy and check that it has not altered quoted documents.

## Checking a unit

- Read the whole new text after corrections. Check the entry from the previous scene, the development and the exit to the next.
- Confirm the facts used, ages, dates, the day of the week, names, forms of address, knowledge and physical availability of objects. Check facts about the past by searching the canonical prose before writing the sentence, not in a summary.
- Check real-world details: travel times and distances, the time of dusk in that month and place, public holidays and office closures. Do not name real companies, churches or hospitals where fiction happens in them ([names and collisions](names-and-collisions.md)).
- When correcting an existing unit, compare length and headings with the previous version.
- Check reveals against earlier volumes and future points of the plan. If the author changes the order of reveals, show the consequences.
- Check the length with the agreed counter, headings, completeness of scenes, separators and workshop leftovers (markers, working brackets, hidden characters). State whether the counter includes headings and dates. The [technical check](technical-check.md) helps.
- Catch unintended repetition, mechanical antitheses and explaining the meaning of a scene after it is already clear from the action. Genre requirements may justify repetition.
- For content based on reality, check the necessary details in reliable sources. Mark unverified details in the report; do not invent citations. Source requirements in non-fiction are part of the text, not only an appendix to the report.

## Checking a module, volume and series

Check the units in scope together, including earlier corrections. Use the error classes from [series validation](series-validation.md) and the correction loop from [modular work](modular-work.md). Cover chronology, the flow of knowledge, the path of documents, characters, props, consequences and the resolution of seeds. Judge whether a breakthrough follows from earlier scenes or needs an event added off the page.

Record results as: location → observation → source of comparison → effect → correction → result of the recheck. Do not mark as "verified" passages that were not read. A finished draft, an automatic audit, an edit and an edition are separate states.
